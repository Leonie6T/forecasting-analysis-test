# Methods and results

**Methods.** We analysed 10,484 probability forecasts from 300 forecasters (60 superforecasters, 80 domain experts, 160 members of the general public) on 40 binary questions across two survey waves. From the raw export we removed 30 exact duplicate rows and 23 forecasts recorded outside the 0 to 1 range, and filled 15 missing outcomes from each question's recorded resolution. Accuracy was measured by the Brier score, computed per forecast, averaged within forecaster, then averaged within group. Uncertainty is reported as a 95% bootstrap confidence interval resampling both forecasters and questions (2,000 draws), since all forecasters answered the same question set. Group differences were tested with Kruskal-Wallis and pairwise Mann-Whitney tests with Holm correction, and confirmed with a mixed model including random intercepts for forecaster and question. The association between years of experience and accuracy was tested within group by Spearman correlation and by OLS with group as a covariate.

**Results.** Mean Brier scores were 0.045 for superforecasters (95% CI 0.039 to 0.051), 0.066 for domain experts (0.058 to 0.075) and 0.114 for the general public (0.105 to 0.125). All groups beat the 0.25 chance benchmark. The groups differed (H = 182, p < 0.001) and every pairwise contrast held after correction (all p < 0.001; Cohen's d 1.4 to 2.6). Results were unchanged across survey waves. Within groups, years of experience was not associated with accuracy (Spearman rho 0.12 to 0.23, all p > 0.05); in a joint model, group explained 52% of variance in Brier scores and experience added nothing (0.0008 per year, p = 0.07). Superforecasters, who averaged fewer years of experience than domain experts, were nonetheless the most accurate group. These are observational associations; the design does not support causal claims about experience.
